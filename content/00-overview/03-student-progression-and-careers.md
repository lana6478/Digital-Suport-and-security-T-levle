# Student Profile, Progression and Careers

*Digital Support and Security T Level → Overview*

This page covers who the qualification is designed for, the job roles it leads into, and the routes students can take after finishing it.

## Student profile

Students taking this Technical Qualification are typically **16–19 years old** and in full-time education. The typical student:

- already has a reasonably clear idea of the industry sector they want a career in, and
- has some idea of the specific type of job role they'd like to explore.

There are no formal entry requirements, but students are more likely to succeed with prior Level 2 attainment (see [Qualification structure](02-qualification-structure.md#entry-requirements-and-assessment-ownership)).

## Alignment to Apprenticeship Standards

The qualification is deliberately aligned to **Level 3 Apprenticeship Standards** so that its content matches real occupations:

- Digital Infrastructure Engineering → *infrastructure technician*
- Network Cabling → *network cable installer*
- Digital Support → *digital support technician*
- Cyber Security → *cyber security technician*

This alignment is what lets the qualification support progression straight into entry-level jobs in Digital Support and Security, not just further study.

## Job roles this qualification leads to

- **Digital support technician**
  - digital applications technician
  - digital service technician
- **Infrastructure technician**
- **IT solutions technician**
  - hardware solutions
  - software solutions
- **Network cable installer**
- **Cyber security technician**

## Progression routes

Students finishing the T Level have several realistic next steps:

1. **Level 3 Apprenticeships** in the areas above, to build toward full occupational competence and gain formal certification of it (the T Level itself only gets students to *threshold* competence - see [Grading and results](04-grading-and-results.md)).
2. **Higher-level Apprenticeships**, depending on the student's skills and experience, such as:
   - Level 4 Digital Infrastructure Engineer
   - Level 4 Network Engineer
   - Level 4 Applications Support Lead
   - Level 4 Cyber Security Technologist
3. **Higher education**, for students who don't have access to an Apprenticeship or who prefer a more academic route:
   - Higher National Certificate (HNC)
   - Higher National Diploma (HND)
   - Degree programmes - students need to check the specific entry requirements of the university/course they're interested in, as these vary by provider.

## Key terms

- **Apprenticeship Standard** - the employer-defined set of knowledge, skills and behaviours for a specific occupation, which T Level Outline Content is built from.
- **Threshold competence** - the level of readiness a T Level gets a student to; full occupational competence is normally reached afterwards, in employment or via an Apprenticeship.
- **HNC / HND** - Higher National Certificate / Diploma, vocational higher education qualifications typically at Level 4/5.
