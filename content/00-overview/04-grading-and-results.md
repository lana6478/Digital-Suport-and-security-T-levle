# Grading, Awarding and Results

*Digital Support and Security T Level → Overview*

This page explains how the Technical Qualification is graded, how those component grades combine into an overall T Level grade, and how results are issued.

## Component grade ranges

| Component | Available grade range |
|---|---|
| Core (Core examinations and Employer Set Project combined) | A\* – E and Unclassified |
| Occupational Specialism | Distinction, Merit, Pass and Unclassified |

The **Core** grade is calculated by aggregating points across all Core assessments (both papers and the ESP) into a single A\*–E grade. For either component, a student whose achievement falls below the minimum standard Pearson judges sufficient receives an **Unclassified (U)**.

## Awarding and grade boundaries

Grade boundaries are set separately for each component/sub-component (Core Examinations, Employer Set Project, Occupational Specialism) in every assessment series, through a process called **awarding**. This exists to keep standards consistent over time, so a student's chance of achieving a given grade doesn't depend on which assessment series (sitting) they happened to take.

## Uniform Mark Scale (UMS)

For the **Core** component, raw marks are converted onto a **Uniform Mark Scale (UMS)**. This benchmarks results across different series so that, for example, a student producing C-grade-standard work on the Employer Set Project gets the same uniform mark as a student doing equivalent work in a different series - regardless of small variations in how hard each series's assessment was.

| Grade | Core Exam (max 240) | Core ESP (max 160) | Core Overall (max 400) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A\* | 216 – 240 | 144 – 160 | 360 – 400 |
| A | 192 – 215 | 128 – 143 | 320 – 359 |
| B | 168 – 191 | 112 – 127 | 280 – 319 |
| C | 144 – 167 | 96 – 111 | 240 – 279 |
| D | 120 – 143 | 80 – 95 | 200 – 239 |
| E | 96 – 119 | 64 – 79 | 160 – 199 |
| U | 0 – 95 | 0 – 63 | 0 – 159 |

Where a student has sat two Core Exams (Paper 1 and Paper 2), their results are combined *before* conversion to UMS.

## Calculating the overall T Level grade

The Department for Education uses a **T Level grade look-up table** (mapping Core UMS + OS grade combinations to an overall T Level grade) to calculate the final result. To be certificated, a student must:

1. Complete **both** components (Core and Occupational Specialism);
2. Achieve at least a **grade E** in the Core;
3. Achieve at least a **Pass** in the Occupational Specialism; and
4. Successfully complete the other elements of the T Level - notably the **industry placement**.

Students who don't meet these minimum requirements are not certificated.

## Results days and reporting

| Assessment series | Results day |
|---|---|
| Summer | August (Level 3 Results Day) |
| November | March (normally the third week - Level 3 Results Day) |

Pearson issues Technical Qualification results directly to Providers, and makes available:

- **Scorecards** - achievement shown as a percentage against each Assessment Objective.
- **Results Plus** - an item-by-item breakdown, letting Providers spot trends within and across cohorts, each item labelled with its Assessment Objective.
- **Statement of Provisional Results** - a watermarked provisional component result slip.

Pearson does not issue Technical Qualification certificates, T Level certificates, or T Level statements of achievement directly - Providers don't need to claim the Technical Qualification separately. Instead:

- The **Department for Education (DfE)** issues overall **T Level results** on Level 3 Results Day in August.
- The DfE provides **T Level certificates** to students who successfully complete every element of the T Level (Core, Occupational Specialism, and industry placement).

## Key terms

- **UMS (Uniform Mark Scale)** - a standardised scale that raw marks are converted onto, to make grades comparable across different assessment series.
- **Awarding** - the process of setting grade boundaries for a given assessment series.
- **Unclassified (U)** - the result given when achievement falls below the minimum standard for a grade.
- **Assessment Objective (AO)** - a specific skill/outcome an assessment is designed to test (see [Core scheme of assessment](../01-core-component/04-core-scheme-of-assessment.md) for the Core AOs).
