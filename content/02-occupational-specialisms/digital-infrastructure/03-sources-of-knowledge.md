# Digital Infrastructure - Sources of Knowledge

*Digital Support and Security T Level → Occupational Specialisms → Digital Infrastructure → Sources of Knowledge*

This page covers Content Area 3 of the Digital Infrastructure specialism: discovering, evaluating and applying reliable sources of knowledge. Every specialism shares this content area, since finding and judging good information is a professional skill in its own right - this version applies it specifically to digital infrastructure work.

## 3.1 Types of sources and legitimising their use

Recognised source types include academic publications, supplier literature (handbooks, device-specific articles), search engines, websites (wikis, forums, Stack Overflow, manufacturer sites), social media, blogs, vlogs, professional networks/conferences, e-learning (MOOCs, vendor qualifications such as Cisco), and peers/colleagues.

To use a source legitimately: clarify what the requirement actually is, pick up to three appropriate sources, apply reliability/validity factors (authority, publication date), assess potential bias, and check the source actually fits the requirement. *(E4, D1.)*

## 3.2 Reliability and validity factors

- **Industry-certified accreditation** (e.g. Cisco CCNA, Microsoft MTA).
- **Appropriateness** to the task.
- **Evidence-based** - supported by citations.
- **Relevant context**.
- **Credibility of the author** - affiliation to recognised bodies, reputation, relevant experience/qualifications.
- **Target audience** - was it written with the right audience in mind (technical vs non-technical language)?
- **Publication** - is it the current version, and how recent is it?

## 3.3 Searching and corroborating information (skill)

Clarify the parameters of the search (e.g. explore trends in the digital economy or Big Data), identify where the required information lives, search safely and securely, cross-reference multiple sources to corroborate findings, and apply reliability/validity/bias checks throughout. *(E4, D5.)*

## 3.4 Identifying bias

- **Author/proprietary bias** - the author's own unweighted opinion.
- **Confirmation bias** - sources chosen because they support an existing assumption.
- **Selection bias** - sources chosen to fit specific criteria.
- **Cultural bias** - assumptions rooted in societal norms.

Bias shows up as partiality, prejudice or omission, and is reduced by grounding conclusions in fact/evidence and taking an inclusive, objective approach that represents demographics fully. Students must be able to name the type of bias present, point to its indicators, and explain both how it arose and how it could be avoided. *(E1, E3, E5, M6, D3.)*

## 3.5 Critical thinking and evaluation (skill)

**Process**: identify relevant information (arguments, views, opinions) → analyse it (spot bias/objectivity, understand how information and data relate) → select suitable evaluation techniques/tools → evaluate findings → record conclusions.

**Techniques**: formative and summative evaluation, qualitative methods (interviews, observation, workshops), quantitative methods (experiments, surveys, statistics), benchmarking, and corroboration (cross-referencing, triangulation).

**Tools**: gap analysis, KPI analysis, scorecards, observation reports, user diaries, scenario mapping, self-assessment frameworks, maturity assessments.

Applied to a digital infrastructure requirement, this means clarifying the evaluation's parameters, choosing and applying the right technique/tool, and recording the findings. *(E1, E3, E4, M5, M6, M8, D2, D3, D4.)*

## 3.6 Communication and knowledge-sharing methods

IT service management tools (incident/problem and change-management systems), knowledge bases, wikis and shared documents, shared digital workspaces, telephone, instant messaging, email, video conferencing, digital signage, social media (organisational, public, personal), blogs, community forums, project management tools (issue logs, Gantt charts, Kanban boards, burndown charts), and policy/process/procedure documents.

## 3.7 Comparing sources and justifying choices (skill)

Identify the sources being compared, apply reliability/validity factors to each, compare the outcomes, and explain - using correct technical terms - which source(s) to rely on and why. *(E1, E3, E5, M5, D3.)*

## Key terms

- **Corroboration** - checking a claim against multiple independent sources.
- **Triangulation** - using three or more sources/methods to validate a finding.
- **RAG-style bias indicators** - partiality, prejudice, omission.
