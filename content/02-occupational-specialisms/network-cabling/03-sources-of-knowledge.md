# Network Cabling - Sources of Knowledge

## 3.1 Types of sources and legitimising their use

Recognised source types: academic publications, supplier literature (handbooks, device-specific articles), search engines, websites (wikis, forums, Stack Overflow, manufacturer sites), social media, blogs, vlogs, professional networks/conferences, e-learning (MOOCs, vendor qualifications such as Cisco), and peers.

To use a source legitimately: clarify the requirement, choose up to three appropriate sources, apply reliability/validity factors, check for bias, and confirm the source actually meets the need. *(E4, D1.)*

## 3.2 Reliability and validity factors

Industry-certified accreditation (e.g. Cisco CCNA, Microsoft MTA), appropriateness, evidence base (citations), relevant context, author credibility (affiliation, reputation, experience/qualifications), audience fit (technical vs non-technical), and publication currency (version, date).

## 3.3 Searching and corroborating information (skill)

Clarify the search parameters, identify where the information lives, search safely and securely, cross-reference multiple sources to corroborate, and apply reliability/validity/bias checks throughout. *(E4, D5.)*

## 3.4 Identifying bias

Author/proprietary bias, confirmation bias, selection bias and cultural bias, shown through partiality, prejudice or omission, and reduced through evidence-based, inclusive, objective practice with full demographic representation. Students identify the type of bias, its indicators, and explain how it arose and how it could be avoided.

## 3.5 Critical thinking and evaluation (skill)

Process: identify relevant information → analyse it (bias, objectivity, how information relates to data) → select evaluation techniques/tools → evaluate findings → record conclusions. Techniques: formative/summative evaluation, qualitative (interviews, observation, workshops) and quantitative (experiments, surveys, statistics) methods, benchmarking, and corroboration (cross-referencing, triangulation). Tools: gap analysis, KPI analysis, scorecards, observation reports, user diaries, scenario mapping, self-assessment frameworks, maturity assessments. *(E1, E3, E4, M5, M6, M8, D2, D3, D4.)*

## 3.6 Communication and knowledge-sharing methods

IT service management tools, knowledge bases, wikis/shared documents, shared digital workspaces, telephone, instant messaging, email, video conferencing, digital signage, social media, blogs, community forums, project management tools (issue logs, Gantt charts, Kanban boards, burndown charts), and policy/process/procedure documents.

## 3.7 Comparing sources and justifying choices (skill)

Identify sources for comparison, apply reliability/validity factors, compare outcomes, and recommend which source(s) to trust using correct technical terms. *(E1, E3, E5, M5, D3.)*

## Key terms

- **Triangulation** - validating a finding using three or more independent sources or methods.
- **Confirmation bias** - favouring sources that support an assumption you already hold.
