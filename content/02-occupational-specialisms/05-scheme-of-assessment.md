# Occupational Specialism: Scheme of Assessment

*Digital Support and Security T Level → Occupational Specialisms → Scheme of Assessment*

Each Occupational Specialism (OS) is assessed by a **single synoptic project** — a substantial, externally-set and externally-marked task that pulls together everything from the specialism's three content areas. The synoptic design matters: it's how Pearson checks that a student can actually combine the security, technical-skill and research strands together to demonstrate *threshold competence*, rather than just recalling isolated facts.

The project is broken into several activities grouped into **three or four substantive tasks**, each completed within a Pearson-set assessment window under supervised conditions (with some tasks allowing unsupervised preparation where appropriate). Across all four specialisms the overall shape is the same:

- **Duration:** 37 hours 30 minutes of externally-assessed project time, run across multiple supervised sessions rather than one sitting.
- **Format:** students respond to a given scenario, producing a portfolio of evidence submitted electronically.
- **Internet access** is permitted for all tasks *except* the final evaluation task.
- **Marking:** externally marked by Pearson.
- Students are **not** assessed against isolated knowledge outcomes — they're expected to draw on and apply relevant underpinning knowledge in service of the practical outcomes.

## Digital Infrastructure

- **99 marks.**
- **Performance Outcomes:** PO1 — apply procedures and controls to maintain digital security; PO2 — explain, install, configure, test and manage physical and virtual infrastructure; PO3 — discover, evaluate and apply reliable sources of knowledge.
- **Tasks:** Task 1 — analysing a problem and designing a solution; Task 2 — developing the solution; Task 3 — evaluating the system.
- **Weighting:** PO1 is the largest single share of marks (45 raw marks, 45.5% of total), ahead of PO2 (34 marks, 34.3%) and PO3 (20 marks, 20.2%) — security procedure and control is the dominant strand even in this infrastructure-focused project.

## Network Cabling

- **111 marks** — the largest mark allocation of the four specialisms, reflecting its heavily practical nature.
- **Performance Outcomes:** PO1 — apply procedures and controls to maintain digital security; PO2 — install and test cabling in line with technical and security requirements; PO3 — discover, evaluate and apply reliable sources of knowledge.
- **Tasks:** Task 1 — analysing a problem and designing a solution; Task 2 — developing the solution; Task 3 — evaluating the network cabling solution.
- **Weighting:** here PO2 dominates (56 marks, 50.5%) — over half the marks sit with the hands-on cabling installation and testing work — with PO1 at 29 marks (26.1%) and PO3 at 26 marks (23.4%).

## Digital Support

- **99 marks.**
- **Performance Outcomes:** PO1 — apply procedures and controls to maintain digital security; PO2 — install, configure and support software applications and operating systems; PO3 — discover, evaluate and apply reliable sources of knowledge.
- **Tasks:** Task 1 — analysing a problem and designing a solution; Task 2 — developing the solution; Task 3 — evaluating the support solutions.
- **Weighting:** PO1 leads narrowly (39 marks, 39.4%), just ahead of PO2 (36 marks, 36.4%) and PO3 (24 marks, 24.2%).

## Cyber Security

- **99 marks.**
- **Performance Outcomes:** PO1 — apply procedures and controls to maintain digital security; PO2 — propose remediation advice for a security risk assessment; PO3 — discover, evaluate and apply reliable sources of knowledge.
- **Tasks:** four parts overall — Task 1 (analysing a problem and designing a solution), Task 2 (developing the solution) and Task 3 (evaluating the system), with the source specification describing this project as having four component parts in total.
- **Weighting:** PO2 carries the most marks (38, 38.4%), narrowly ahead of PO1 (35 marks, 35.4%) and PO3 (26 marks, 26.3%) — remediation and risk-assessment skill is the single biggest strand, closely followed by security procedures and controls.

## Grading

Each OS project is graded **Pass, Merit, Distinction or Unclassified**, based on the total marks achieved against the published mark scheme for that specialism. This OS grade then combines with the graded core component (Core Papers 1 & 2 and the Employer Set Project, each graded A\*–E or Unclassified) to produce the overall T Level grade of Pass, Merit, Distinction, Distinction\* or Unclassified — see [Grading and Results](../00-overview/04-grading-and-results.md) for the full calculation.

Administratively, providers running any of these projects must follow the General Administrative Support Guide and the Technical-Qualification-specific Administration Support Guide for the OS project, both published on Pearson's Training and Admin Support webpage.

## Delivery resources

Providers must be equipped to deliver whichever specialism they teach. Across all four specialisms, the baseline requirement is broadly the same:

- **General computing:** up-to-date PCs or Macs capable of running Cisco Packet Tracer, office productivity software and a web browser; word-processing and spreadsheet software; internet access; a printer.
- **Installation/configuration environment:** either physical standalone desktops/laptops or a virtualised environment for installing and configuring systems, provided it can't compromise other live systems — plus equipment for capturing or generating media assets as evidence.
- **Staffing:** teachers with relevant digital-sector qualifications or experience, and a curriculum team whose combined expertise spans the full breadth of the qualification content.

On top of that shared baseline, each specialism has its own specific kit:

- **Digital Infrastructure:** network diagramming software (Packet Tracer or equivalent able to read `.pkt` files); operating systems and end-user/server software for virtualised or physical network builds; anti-virus and firewall software; virtualisation-capable computers and desktop virtualisation software; USB drives/external hard drives; WiFi access.
- **Network Cabling:** everything Digital Infrastructure needs, *plus* physical cabling hardware — UTP cable, RJ45 connectors, wall sockets, a patch panel, termination tools (wire cutters, crimping tools), testing tools (cable tester, tone generator and probe), a labelling machine, cable management fixtures (trunking, ties), a network switch, a router and a WiFi access point.
- **Digital Support:** the same networking/virtualisation baseline, plus a wider spread of end-user support tools — a mobile device (tablet or smartphone), anti-virus software, a PDF reader, an alternative web browser, email client software and intranet software.
- **Cyber Security:** the networking/virtualisation baseline plus a security-specific toolset — anti-virus and anti-malware software, vulnerability scanning software, firewall software, and word-processing software geared toward producing written evidence/reports (reflecting the report-heavy nature of the risk-assessment work).
